# Nori

"A thin layer that holds everything together."

# The Goal

Empower chefs(builders, makers, manufacturers, etc) to visualize, share, and continuously improve their standard operating procedures (SOPs) using Kanban style workflows.

# The Story

Noro, is the name of our favorite fictional sushi restaurant. They boast a menu of hundreds of different sushi rolls. Everything from the simplest california roll to the most complex of master sushi rolls.

Nora uses Nori to process orders, help chefs keep their sanity, improve productivity, and overall customer satisfaction.

But how does it work?

A happy couple sits down for a date and is greeted by a friendly waitress, Nora. Nora, suggests today's special, "Fresh Salmon Nigiri." and then proceeds to take the couple's order. The couple orders an appetizer of "Fried Pickled Calamari", an order of the day's special, 2 tiger rolls, and 1 spicy tuna roll, leaving room for desert of course.

Nora takes their order using "Nori" sending the tickets to the chefs who get started on the order right away.

The order shows up in Nori as two tickets tied to the same order. One, for the appetizer and the second for the rest of the order. Both are placed in the "Todo" column of the Kanban board. The su chef takes the ticket for the fried calamari while the master chef gets started on the rest of the order.

Su chef opens the fried calamari ticket. This ticket was created from the "Fried Pickled SOP", essentially a fork of the SOP. Su chef hits the "start task button" and the task is automatically moved into the "in progress column" with his name attached.

This task is actually comprised of several sub tasks that need to be completed in a specific order.

The SOP has a section for "Materials" (Ingredients) and "Equipment".
Materials show up as single line items that can be expanded if the chef needs more info.
Under "Materials" is the following:

1. Fried Calamari:

- Required amount: 1 Cup.
- Location: Top Shelf of fridge #1.
- Pull.
  This ticket has a "Pull" button the chef can use when they are down to 2 jars of pickled calamari. This adds a "Make Picked Calamari" ticket to the Bottom of the "Todo" column. It is tagged with a tag "prep" so the chefs know that it can be done before the restaurant opens, rather than being tied to an urgent order. There are still 3 jars left in the fridge so they are good for now.

2. Flour:

- Required amount: 1/2 Cup.
- Location: Baking Shelf.
- Pull: Less than 4 bags. Pulling, adds an "Order" task that gets added to "todo". This the manager can use the next morning when putting in orders.

3. Whole Milk

- Amount: 1/4 Cup
- Location: Fridge #2 Middle Shelf
- Pull: Less than 3 gallons.

4. Egg

- Amount: 1
- Location: Fridge #2. Bottom shelf.
- Pull: Less than 6 dozen.

5. Panko Bread crumbs:

- Required amount: 1/2 cup.
- Location: Baking shelf 1.2
- Pull: less than 3 canisters.

6. Fry basket.

- location underneath plating table

7. Fry basket paper.

- Location: Underneath Plating table.

8. 3 Stainless steel bowls. (this should maybe be #1? If chef decides to switch it he can simply drag and drop.)
   Under "Procedure" is the following.

Under Procedure:

1. Toss pickled calamari in bowl of flour.
2. Whisk egg and milk in second bowl.
3. Toss floured calamari in egg mixture. Leave excess flour in first bowl.
4. Toss calamari in panko bread crumbs in bowl number 3.
5. Fry calamari in deep fryer for 5 minutes until golden brown.
6. While calamari is frying fill charger with special dipping sauce.

- Pull when only one bottle is left.

6. Plate on fry paper in fry basket.

Su chef is moving fast, so he should be able to with the click of a single button click "start" on the first procedure step, Then a second click "tap because he's on an ipad" to hit complete. This should automatically log the time it took to complete the step and change the state of the next step to "in progress". There should be a middle button to pause the current step.

While the su chef is taking the first task, the chef gets started on the rest of the order.
Because there are two orders of the california rolls, he drags that to the top of the queue and gets started there. His tasks look similar to the calamari task above.

The spicy tuna roll is pretty easy so he goes ahead and assigns that to Su chef. Su chef can pick up that task once he's sent out the calamari.

Once Chef completes the california rolls, he gets started on the special.

Since this is the first night this special has been used, the SOP is fairly unpolished. Midway through making them, chef realizes there is a better way to make them and wants to update the SOP.

He updates the task to his liking. After doing so, Nori asks him if he would like to update the original SOP. He accepts and Nori merges the changes into the original SOP. This way when they get the next order the SOP has been updated.

Nori should also update any tasks of this type that were opened between the time the first order was taken and the time the SOP was updated.

Nori is optimized for speed. Experienced chefs don't need to look at every little detail of the process, but inexperienced chefs need more guiding. So the sub tasks are expandable and can show pictures, videos, and links to other things.

Nori, is rooted in Lean Manufacturing methodology and should be optimized for flow.

It should help managers find bottle necks, get an idea of WIP. As well schedule maintenance tasks on a timely schedule (floors need to be swept and mopped every night.). Frying oil needs to be changed once a week.

All of these tasks can have tags that let employees filter their board depending on what they are doing. Mornings the team practices 3 S's (sweep, sort, standardize) (3s tags), then goes into prep (prep tag), then once they open chefs start taking tasks tagged (order) and taking prep tasks from the back log when all orders have been caught up.

All of this sushi talk is a metaphor. Nori software should be able to work for a sushi restaurant as well as a furniture manufacturer. I own furniture company and will use this to document SOP's for making furniture.

When creating SOP's I also want there to be a "marketplace" of SOP's. If I make and SOP for tuning my jointer, i'd like to make this public so other companies can fork my SOP. There's really no need to reinvent the wheel. Everyone changes the saw blade on a dewalt miter saw the same way.

Nori should be clean an professional built for productivity, but should have a hint of playful japanese sushi branding.
